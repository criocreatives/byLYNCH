This notice identifies tracking technologies that may be involved in browsing byLYNCH and explains available choices. It supplements the Privacy Policy.

Last updated: 15 October 2026

1. What cookies and similar technologies do

Cookies are small browser-stored records. Sites and third parties may also use pixels, scripts, local storage or comparable technologies for authentication, analytics, advertising and media playback. The presence or category of a cookie may vary according to browser settings, provider configuration, location and whether media or ads load.

2. Technologies found in the website configuration

  • Hosting / ordinary website delivery: GitHub Pages serves files; requests can produce network and server metadata. Hosting-related logging is not necessarily a browser cookie.
  • Google AdSense: The site-managed advertising script is held back until a visitor actively chooses “Allow advertising” on an eligible page. Mature galleries flagged by the publication exclude that script. Google and participating vendors may use cookies and identifiers after loading.
  • Google Fonts: Stylesheets request fonts from Google's servers, which can receive technical request information; this is not itself proof of an advertising cookie.
  • Embedded players: Article pages with YouTube and SoundCloud players display a click-to-load placeholder instead of contacting those providers immediately. The provider may process technical data and set identifiers after you load a player.
  • Local site search: The page's search function fetches a public search index and filters it in the browser; no dedicated tracking cookie was identified in its reviewed source code.

Links to Instagram, Flickr, Gumroad or other outside sites lead to separately operated services; their own tracking policies apply when you follow those links.

3. Advertising choices

Google and its advertising partners may use information from visits to this and other sites to provide and measure advertisements, subject to applicable law and account configurations. To manage Google's interest-based advertising, visit Google My Ad Center. Some industry opt-out mechanisms are described at YourAdChoices. An advertising preference does not necessarily prevent all cookies, network requests or advertising.

4. Browser controls and site consent status

You can configure your browser to block or clear cookies and site data. Private browsing, browser-based content blocking and service-specific privacy settings provide additional controls, but can affect media or site features.

Interim advertising preference: On first visit, a byLYNCH panel offers “Decline optional ads” or “Allow advertising”. Optional AdSense script loading is off unless allowed. Your advertising choice is kept in your browser's local storage as bylynch-ad-choice-v1; it can be changed through “Privacy choices” in the footer. Declining after ads have already loaded reloads the page to prevent further site-initiated ad loading on that visit. Clearing site storage resets the preference.

Certified CMP still required: this locally built preference panel is not Google-certified, does not transmit an IAB TCF consent signal, and is not a substitute for a compliant publisher consent management platform where required. The publication must configure a certified CMP in its AdSense account for applicable EEA, UK and Swiss advertising traffic before relying on this arrangement for regulatory compliance. The site's Google Fonts CSS request still contacts Google servers independently of the advertising choice.

5. Questions and changes

For questions about the publication's use of technologies, make a short “Privacy / byLYNCH” request through @lebosslynch on Instagram without sending sensitive documents. Privacy policies for Google's own products are at Google Privacy Policy; the site cannot modify settings on independently operated platforms.

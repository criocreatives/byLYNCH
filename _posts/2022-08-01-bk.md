<!-- /wp:quote -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Kendrick has a modern-day take on&nbsp;<a href="https://genius.com/artists/2pac">2Pac</a>’s feminist anthem&nbsp;<a href="https://genius.com/2pac-keep-ya-head-up-lyrics/">“Keep Ya Head Up.”</a>&nbsp;While both songs have roots in&nbsp;<a href="https://genius.com/artists/Wallace-thurman">Wallace Thurman</a>’s novel&nbsp;<em>The Blacker the Berry</em>, 2Pac’s version expands on the&nbsp;<a href="https://genius.com/30072">idea that black women are beautiful</a>.&nbsp;</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The original phrase, “the blacker the berry, the sweeter the juice,” was a positive affirmation of blackness. This phrase was also referenced by the Harlem Renaissance novelist Wallace Thurman in the title of&nbsp;<em>The Blacker the Berry: A Novel of Negro Life</em>.</p>
